About This Tiger & Lion Safari

This tiger and lion safari in India brings together two of the world's great cats on one western India journey. In Ranthambore, tigers move through dry forest and past the lakes and pavilions of a thousand-year-old fort. In Gir, on the Saurashtra peninsula of Gujarat, lives the last wild population of the Asiatic lion anywhere on earth, a species that numbered only a handful at the end of the 19th century and has grown to 891 lions in the 2025 estimate.

Every safari is taken in a private 4x4 jeep, reserved for your party alone, with naturalists who know each forest intimately. You stay beside both forests, from a lodge or tented camp on the edge of Ranthambore to a retreat close to Gir's mango orchards and teak. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and domestic flights make every transfer effortless.

Every journey is curated for you alone. Choose any hotel on our list in each destination, and your programme stays the same while the lodges change to suit the way you like to travel. Each journey contributes to TOFTigers, winner of a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025, supporting responsible tiger tourism and the communities beside the forest. In Gir, the lions' recovery owes much to the Maldhari herders who have shared the forest with them for generations. The best time to travel is November to April.

Ranthambore & Gir Itinerary: Day by Day

Safaris included: five private 4x4 jeep safaris with an expert naturalist, one a day, alternating between morning and afternoon: two in Ranthambore (a morning safari on Day 3 and an afternoon safari on Day 4) and three in Gir (a morning safari on Day 7, an afternoon safari on Day 8 and a morning safari on Day 9). If time allows during the day and park tickets are available, we can add further safaris at an additional cost.

Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

A VIP representative meets you at the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur drives you to your hotel, where the rest of the day is yours to rest after your flight.

Day 2: New Delhi to Ranthambore National Park

Drive: about 3 to 3.5 hours (160 km) from Jaipur

Drive: about 3 to 3.5 hours (160 km) from Jaipur. A short morning flight takes you to Jaipur, where your chauffeur is waiting for the drive through the Rajasthan countryside to Ranthambore. Your lodge welcomes you with lunch, then the afternoon is yours to settle in. In the evening, dine beneath the stars.

Day 3: Ranthambore National Park

A morning safari takes you through dry forest, rocky ridges and grassland to the lakes of Padam Talao, Rajbagh and Malik Talao, where tigers come to drink and sambar wade among the water plants. Your naturalist follows alarm calls and fresh pugmarks, while mugger crocodiles bask on the banks and leopards rest on the rocky slopes above. The afternoon is at leisure.

Day 4: Ranthambore National Park

Spend the morning at leisure at your lodge, or visit Ranthambore Fort, part of the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan, where the walls and temples look out across the forest. In the afternoon, a final safari returns you to the lakes and ruined pavilions in the golden light.

Day 5: Ranthambore to New Delhi

Drive: about 3 to 3.5 hours (160 km) to Jaipur, then a flight of about 1 hour

Drive: about 3 to 3.5 hours (160 km) to Jaipur, then a flight of about 1 hour. After breakfast, drive back to Jaipur for your flight to Delhi. Tonight you stay in Delhi, ready for tomorrow's flight west.

Day 6: New Delhi to Gir National Park

Drive: about 4 hours (190 km) from Rajkot, after a flight of about 1 hour 50 minutes

Drive: about 4 hours (190 km) from Rajkot, after a flight of about 1 hour 50 minutes. A morning flight carries you to Rajkot, in the Saurashtra peninsula of Gujarat, and your chauffeur drives you south through farmland and mango orchards to Gir. Your lodge lies on the edge of the forest. Spend the evening by the pool or on your veranda as peafowl call from the trees.

Day 7: Gir National Park

Set out at dawn on the Gir Jungle Trail, following forest roads through teak, scrub and grassland in search of lions. Prides often rest in the open or beside the track, and your naturalist explains how the population has recovered from just a handful of animals. Leopards, striped hyenas, chital, nilgai and chinkara also share these forests. The afternoon is at leisure at your lodge.

Day 8: Gir National Park

Begin the morning with a visit to a Maldhari settlement, where herders who have lived alongside the lions for generations share their way of life. In the afternoon, a safari takes you deep into the sanctuary to Kamleshwar Dam on the Hiran River, where mugger crocodiles bask on the banks. Dinner tonight is served outdoors in the grounds of your lodge.

Day 9: Gir National Park and Somnath

Enjoy a final morning safari in Gir. In the afternoon, drive about an hour to the coast at Somnath, where the great temple stands above the Arabian Sea, and stay for the evening aarti as lamps are lit at dusk.

Day 10: Gir to New Delhi

Drive: about 4 hours (190 km) to Rajkot, then a flight of about 1 hour 50 minutes

Drive: about 4 hours (190 km) to Rajkot, then a flight of about 1 hour 50 minutes. After a leisurely breakfast, drive back to Rajkot for your flight to Delhi. Your final evening is in the capital.

Day 11: Departure

Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

A note from our planners: Gir National Park is open from 16 October to 15 June, so this journey runs between those months. Safaris in both parks follow routes and permits set by the forest department, and we secure them well in advance.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves
  • Ranthambore National Park: dry forest, rocky ridges and lakes in eastern Rajasthan, beneath a 10th-century fort that forms part of the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan. It is home to some of India's most photographed tigers.
  • Gir National Park and Wildlife Sanctuary: about 1,410 sq km of teak forest, scrub and grassland in Gujarat, the only place in the world where wild Asiatic lions live. Once the hunting ground of the Nawab of Junagadh, it became a sanctuary in 1965, and the 2025 estimate recorded 891 lions across the wider Saurashtra landscape.

UNESCO World Heritage Sites
  • Ranthambore Fort: one of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan, inscribed as a World Heritage Site in 2013, standing above the forest on a rocky hilltop.

Tiger & Asiatic Lion Safari FAQs

When is the best time for a tiger and lion safari in India?

November to April. Gir is open from 16 October to 15 June. November to March brings cool, comfortable days, while April is warmer and often excellent for sightings, as animals gather near water.

What are my chances of seeing lions and tigers?

Lion sightings in Gir are frequent, as prides often rest in the open beside the track, and Ranthambore is one of the best places in India to see a tiger. Wild animals can never be guaranteed, but with three nights in Ranthambore and four in Gir, you give yourself every chance.

How do we travel between Ranthambore and Gir?

The two parks lie in different states, so the simplest route is through Delhi. You fly from Jaipur to Delhi, spend a night in the capital, then take a morning flight to Rajkot and drive about four hours to Gir.

Is this tiger and lion safari suitable for families?

Yes. Both forests welcome families, and the shorter guided drives at Devalia in Gir suit children who are new to safaris. Our safari specialists can adjust the pace for your group.

How do I book a private tiger and lion safari?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will curate this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private and bespoke, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead for the best choice of lodges and park permits.
