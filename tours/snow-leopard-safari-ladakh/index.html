About This Snow Leopard Safari

This snow leopard safari in Ladakh takes you into the high, silent valleys of the Indian Himalaya in the depths of winter, when the elusive "ghost of the mountains" follows its prey down from the peaks. Ladakh is one of the best places in the world to see a wild snow leopard, and two of its finest areas anchor this journey: the high village of Ulley in the Sham valley, and the Rumbak valley in Hemis National Park, where blue sheep graze on slopes the cats patrol.

Each day is shaped by the spotters, local men and women who scan the ridges from first light with powerful scopes and guide you to a vantage point the moment a cat is found. You spend three nights in Rumbak, the heart of Hemis, giving you more time in the field than on our shorter extension. Two nights at The Grand Dragon Ladakh in Leh let you adjust gently to the altitude, while Snow Leopard Lodge and House of Rumbak offer warm, intimate comfort in the mountains. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and domestic flights make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Snow leopard tourism gives Ladakh's mountain villages a lasting stake in protecting the cats, employing local people as spotters, hosts and cooks. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is January to March, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

What You Will Experience

  • Three nights in the Rumbak valley, in Hemis National Park, one of the finest snow leopard landscapes on earth.

Snow Leopard Safari in Ladakh Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    A VIP representative meets you at the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur drives you the few minutes to Roseate House in Aerocity, perfectly placed for tomorrow's early flight to Leh. Rest, enjoy a quiet dinner and turn in early.

    Overnight: Roseate House, New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi to Leh

    An early flight carries you north across the main Himalayan range, with snow-covered peaks stretching below the wing, to Leh, the capital of Ladakh. You are met on arrival and driven the short distance to The Grand Dragon Ladakh. Leh lies at about 3,500 metres (11,500 feet), so today is for rest, warm drinks and plenty of water as your body adjusts.

    Overnight: The Grand Dragon Ladakh, Leh

  3. Day 3: Leh

    A gentle day of acclimatisation. In the morning, visit Spituk Monastery on its hill above the Indus, then wander the lanes of the old town beneath Leh Palace. Along the frozen edges of the Indus, your naturalist looks for the ibisbill, a rare wader that winters on Ladakh's rivers. In the evening, a briefing introduces the snow leopard, its prey and the work of the spotters.

    Overnight: The Grand Dragon Ladakh, Leh

  4. Day 4: Leh to Ulley

    Drive west along the Indus for two to three hours, pausing at Nimmu where the turquoise Zanskar meets the Indus, before climbing into the Sham valley to Ulley, at almost 4,000 metres (13,000 feet). Snow Leopard Lodge is small, warm and devoted entirely to wildlife, with wood stoves and hearty home-cooked meals. In the afternoon, join the spotters for your first scan of the ridges around the village.

    Overnight: Snow Leopard Lodge, Ulley

  5. Day 5: Ulley

    At first light, the spotters fan out across the valley. Ulley's cliffs are home to herds of Himalayan ibex and urial, and the cats that hunt them, and wolves and red foxes are often seen crossing the slopes. When a snow leopard is found, you are guided to a vantage point where the scopes bring it close. Hot lunches and warm stoves await at the lodge between excursions.

    Overnight: Snow Leopard Lodge, Ulley

  6. Day 6: Ulley to Rumbak, Hemis National Park

    After a final morning scan, drive back past Leh and into Hemis National Park, about four hours in all. From the end of the road, a short walk beside a frozen stream brings you to House of Rumbak Wildlife Lodge, just below Rumbak village, where six heated cottages in traditional Ladakhi style look out over snow-dusted peaks.

    Overnight: House of Rumbak Wildlife Lodge, Hemis National Park

  7. Day 7: Rumbak, Hemis National Park

    Spend the day with the spotters in the Rumbak valley, where herds of blue sheep graze the slopes and their nervous movements often betray a hunting cat. Bearded vultures and golden eagles ride the thermals overhead. Lunch is served in the field or back at the lodge, depending on where the day leads.

    Overnight: House of Rumbak Wildlife Lodge, Hemis National Park

  8. Day 8: Rumbak, Hemis National Park

    A third day in Rumbak gives you the best chance of the sightings you have come for, and perhaps a second encounter for photographers. In the afternoon, walk up to Rumbak village to share butter tea with a local family and learn how life is lived through the long mountain winter. Your farewell dinner is served beside the wood-fired stove.

    Overnight: House of Rumbak Wildlife Lodge, Hemis National Park

  9. Day 9: Rumbak to New Delhi

    After an early breakfast, return to Leh, about an hour away, for your flight to Delhi and your onward journey.

A note from our planners

Leh lies at about 3,500 metres and Ulley at almost 4,000 metres, so the two nights in Leh are essential for acclimatisation. Winter nights can fall to minus 20°C, though the lodges are warm and the days are often bright and sunny. Flights to and from Leh can be delayed by winter weather, so we recommend a spare night in Delhi before your international flight home.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Hemis National Park: A vast high-altitude park in eastern Ladakh, established in 1981 and among the largest in India. Its Rumbak and Markha valleys are renowned as some of the best snow leopard habitat in the world, rich in blue sheep, Tibetan wolves and birds of prey.
  • Ulley and the Sham Valley: A high village west of Leh, beyond the protected areas, where community conservation shelters herds of ibex and urial, and the snow leopards and wolves that follow them.

Snow Leopard Safari in Ladakh FAQs

When is the best time for a snow leopard safari in Ladakh?

January to March. Deep winter snow pushes blue sheep and ibex down the valleys, and the snow leopards follow them to lower slopes where the spotters can find them.

What are my chances of seeing a snow leopard?

Snow leopards are wild and famously elusive, so a sighting can never be guaranteed. With six days in the field across Ulley and Rumbak, two of the finest areas in Ladakh, and the most experienced spotters, your chances are very good.

How cold is it, and how fit do I need to be?

Winter nights can fall to minus 20°C, but days are often bright and the lodges are warm. Most viewing is from vantage points close to the road or lodge, with short walks at altitude, so a reasonable level of fitness is all you need. We provide a detailed packing list when you book.

Is this snow leopard tour suitable for everyone?

It is best suited to adults and older children in good health. Leh lies at about 3,500 metres and Ulley at almost 4,000 metres, so we build in two nights to acclimatise and recommend speaking with your doctor before you travel.

How do I book a private snow leopard tour in India?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will tailor this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private, bespoke and curated for you, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead, as the lodges in Ulley and Rumbak have only a handful of rooms.
