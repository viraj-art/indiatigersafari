About This Tiger Safari & Birding Tour

This tiger safari and birding tour in India pairs the tigers of Ranthambore with two of the country's finest places for birds. Ranthambore, where tigers stalk among the ruins of a thousand-year-old fort, is also rich in raptors, owls and forest birds. Keoladeo National Park at Bharatpur, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, is one of the world's great wetland sanctuaries, alive in winter with cranes, storks, pelicans and waterfowl from as far away as Siberia. On the Chambal River, a quiet boat carries you past basking gharials, river dolphins and the elegant Indian skimmer.

You stay at Aman-i-Khas, a camp of luxury tents on the edge of Ranthambore; Laxmi Vilas Palace, a former royal residence of the Jat rulers of Bharatpur, just 2 km from the bird sanctuary; and the Chambal Safari Lodge, a family-run heritage lodge in woodland near the river. Tiger safaris are taken in a private 4x4 jeep, reserved for your party alone, and expert birding naturalists guide you at Keoladeo and on the Chambal. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and a domestic flight make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Our partner lodges employ and train people from neighbouring villages, our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting, and your park fees help protect the reserves themselves. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is November to March, when the migrant birds are in residence, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

What You Will Experience

  • Tigers in Ranthambore, where they roam among lakes, banyan trees and the ruins of a hilltop fort.
  • Private 4x4 jeep safaris at dawn and dusk, reserved for your party alone and led by expert naturalists.
  • Keoladeo National Park, a UNESCO-listed wetland famed for its sarus cranes, painted storks and winter waterfowl.
  • A boat safari on the Chambal, home to the critically endangered gharial, Gangetic river dolphins and the Indian skimmer.
  • Expert birding naturalists who know every call, nest and roost along the way.
  • Aman-i-Khas, a camp of luxury tents on the edge of Ranthambore, with heritage stays at Laxmi Vilas Palace and the Chambal Safari Lodge.
  • An optional stop at the Taj Mahal on your final drive back to Delhi.

Ranthambore, Keoladeo & Chambal Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    A VIP representative meets you at the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur drives you to The Oberoi New Delhi, where you can rest after your flight. If you arrive early, the hotel gardens and nearby Lodhi Gardens are a gentle introduction to India's common birds.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi to Ranthambore National Park

    A short morning flight takes you to Jaipur, where your chauffeur is waiting for the drive of about three hours to Ranthambore. Aman-i-Khas, a camp of spacious tents inspired by the Mughal encampments of old, lies on the edge of the reserve. In the afternoon, take a birding walk around the camp with your naturalist, watching for Indian peafowl, grey francolin and the Indian robin.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  3. Day 3: Ranthambore National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore Ranthambore's dry forest, rocky ridges and lakes, where tigers are seen beside ancient pavilions and the water of Padam Talao. The reserve is also excellent for birds: crested serpent eagles, brown fish owls, painted spurfowl and Indian paradise flycatchers are all found here, and your naturalist balances the search for tigers with time for birds.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  4. Day 4: Ranthambore National Park

    After a dawn safari, spend the late morning at leisure in camp. In the afternoon, choose between a final safari or a visit to Ranthambore Fort, part of the UNESCO-listed Hill Forts of Rajasthan, where vultures and raptors ride the thermals above the walls and the views stretch across the forest.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  5. Day 5: Ranthambore to Bharatpur

    After breakfast, drive north-east for four to five hours through the farmland of eastern Rajasthan to Bharatpur. Laxmi Vilas Palace, once a royal residence of Bharatpur's Jat rulers and still home to the family, welcomes you with gilded halls, courtyards and wide lawns, just 2 km from the sanctuary. In the late afternoon, take your first visit to Keoladeo National Park with your birding naturalist.

    Overnight: Laxmi Vilas Palace, Bharatpur

  6. Day 6: Keoladeo National Park

    A full day in Keoladeo, once the duck-shooting reserve of the Maharajas of Bharatpur and now one of the world's great bird sanctuaries. Explore its raised paths on foot, by bicycle or in a cycle rickshaw driven by a local guide trained in birdwatching. Sarus cranes, painted storks, bar-headed geese, pelicans and a host of ducks crowd the wetlands in winter, while dusky eagle-owls, spotted owlets and Indian rock pythons can be found along the drier trails.

    Overnight: Laxmi Vilas Palace, Bharatpur

  7. Day 7: Bharatpur to the Chambal

    After an early walk in Keoladeo, drive about three hours to the Chambal Safari Lodge, a heritage lodge in woodland near the Chambal River, built by local landowners and still run by their family. In the afternoon, a boat safari on the Chambal brings you close to gharials basking on sandbanks, mugger crocodiles and Gangetic river dolphins surfacing in the current. Indian skimmers, black-bellied terns and great thick-knees work the river's edge.

    Overnight: Chambal Safari Lodge

  8. Day 8: National Chambal Sanctuary

    Spend a second morning on the river, when the light is soft and the birds most active. In the afternoon, explore the ravines on a camel safari or visit the riverside temples of Bateshwar on the Yamuna. Your farewell dinner is served in the lodge's gardens beneath old trees.

    Overnight: Chambal Safari Lodge

  9. Day 9: Chambal to New Delhi

    After a final morning birding walk, drive about five hours to Delhi. If you wish, stop in Agra on the way to see the Taj Mahal (closed on Fridays). Your final evening is at The Oberoi New Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  10. Day 10: Departure

    Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

A note from our planners

The route runs from Ranthambore to Keoladeo and the Chambal so that each drive is short and you end within easy reach of Delhi and Agra. Ranthambore's safaris follow zones set by the forest department, and the Chambal Safari Lodge is open from October to April.

Birds You May See: Ranthambore, Keoladeo & Chambal

This route is one of the richest birding circuits in India, moving from dry forest to wetland to river. These are among the species our naturalists record most often; sightings vary with the season and water levels.

Ranthambore National Park

  • Raptors and owls: Crested Serpent Eagle, Changeable Hawk-Eagle, Bonelli's Eagle, Shikra, Black-winged Kite, Indian Vulture, Red-headed Vulture, Egyptian Vulture, Brown Fish Owl, Indian Eagle-Owl, Spotted Owlet
  • Forest and scrub birds: Indian Peafowl, Painted Spurfowl, Grey Francolin, Painted Sandgrouse, Indian Grey Hornbill, Indian Paradise Flycatcher, White-browed Fantail, Sirkeer Malkoha, Plum-headed Parakeet, Alexandrine Parakeet, Yellow-footed Green Pigeon, Indian Golden Oriole, Rufous Treepie, Yellow-crowned Woodpecker, Small Minivet, Indian Roller, Crested Bunting, Indian Pitta (summer)
  • Lakes and waterholes: Black Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, Asian Openbill, Great Thick-knee, Bar-headed Goose, White-throated Kingfisher, Pied Kingfisher

Keoladeo National Park

  • Cranes, storks and ibises: Sarus Crane, Common Crane, Painted Stork, Black-necked Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, Asian Openbill, Eurasian Spoonbill, Black-headed Ibis, Glossy Ibis, Red-naped Ibis
  • Pelicans, cormorants and herons: Great White Pelican, Oriental Darter, Great Cormorant, Indian Cormorant, Little Cormorant, Grey Heron, Purple Heron, Black-crowned Night Heron, Indian Pond Heron, Great Egret, Intermediate Egret, Little Egret, Yellow Bittern
  • Ducks and geese: Bar-headed Goose, Greylag Goose, Knob-billed Duck, Lesser Whistling Duck, Cotton Pygmy Goose, Ruddy Shelduck, Indian Spot-billed Duck, Gadwall, Eurasian Wigeon, Northern Shoveler, Northern Pintail, Common Teal, Garganey, Common Pochard, Ferruginous Duck, Tufted Duck
  • Rails, jacanas and waders: Grey-headed Swamphen, Bronze-winged Jacana, Pheasant-tailed Jacana, Black-winged Stilt, Red-wattled Lapwing, White-tailed Lapwing, Common Snipe, Wood Sandpiper, Green Sandpiper
  • Raptors and owls: Greater Spotted Eagle, Indian Spotted Eagle, Eastern Imperial Eagle, Booted Eagle, Western Marsh Harrier, Osprey, Dusky Eagle-Owl, Spotted Owlet, Indian Scops Owl
  • Woodland birds: Black Francolin, Coppersmith Barbet, Brown-headed Barbet, Black-rumped Flameback, Bluethroat, Black Redstart, Red-breasted Flycatcher, Greater Coucal, Green Bee-eater, Common Kingfisher

National Chambal Sanctuary

  • River specialists: Indian Skimmer, Black-bellied Tern, River Tern, Little Tern, Great Thick-knee, Small Pratincole, River Lapwing, Pallas's Gull, Brown-headed Gull
  • Waterbirds: Bar-headed Goose, Ruddy Shelduck, Sarus Crane, Black-necked Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, Red-naped Ibis, Pied Kingfisher
  • Birds of the ravines: Indian Courser, Bonelli's Eagle, Egyptian Vulture, Brown Rock Chat, Sand Lark, Indian Peafowl

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Ranthambore National Park: Dry forest, rocky ridges and lakes beneath a 10th-century fort in eastern Rajasthan, home to some of India's most photographed tigers and over 300 species of birds.
  • Keoladeo National Park: A compact wetland at Bharatpur, once a royal duck-shooting reserve, inscribed as a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 1985. More than 350 bird species have been recorded here.
  • National Chambal Sanctuary: A long protected stretch of the Chambal River shared by Rajasthan, Madhya Pradesh and Uttar Pradesh, and the world's most important stronghold for the critically endangered gharial.

Tiger Safari & Birding Tour FAQs

When is the best time for a tiger safari and birding tour in India?

November to March is ideal. Migrant waterfowl and cranes fill the wetlands of Keoladeo, Indian skimmers and bar-headed geese gather on the Chambal, and the days are cool and clear for safaris in Ranthambore.

What birds and wildlife can I expect to see?

At Keoladeo, sarus cranes, painted storks, pelicans, owls and many species of duck and geese. On the Chambal, gharials, river dolphins, Indian skimmers and river terns. In Ranthambore, tigers, leopards and sloth bears, along with raptors and forest birds. Wild animals are never guaranteed, but this route offers exceptional variety.

Do I need to be an experienced birdwatcher?

Not at all. Our naturalists tailor each outing to your interest, whether you are a keen birder with a long target list or simply enjoy watching wildlife. Binoculars are provided on safari, though we recommend bringing your own if you have a favourite pair.

Is this tour suitable for families?

Yes. Distances are short, the boat safaris and cycle rickshaw rides are gentle, and the variety of animals and birds keeps younger travellers engaged. Our safari specialists can adjust the pace to suit your group.

How do I book a private tiger safari and birding tour?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will tailor this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private, bespoke and curated for you, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead for the best choice of camps and park permits in the winter season.
