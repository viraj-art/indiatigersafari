About This Golden Triangle Tour

This Golden Triangle tour with tiger safari brings together India's two great icons: the monuments of its Mughal and Rajput rulers, and the Bengal tiger. Over ten unhurried days you explore Delhi, watch the sun rise over the Taj Mahal and wander the palaces of Jaipur, then head into Ranthambore, where tigers roam among the ruins of a thousand-year-old fort.

In each city, a private historian brings the monuments to life, with early starts timed to beat the crowds and afternoons left free to rest or explore. You stay at The Oberoi New Delhi, The Oberoi Amarvilas, where every room looks out to the Taj Mahal, and Rambagh Palace, once the home of the Maharaja of Jaipur. The journey ends with three nights at Aman-i-Khas, an intimate camp of ten luxury tents on the edge of Ranthambore, with private jeep safaris each morning and afternoon. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport and chauffeur-driven private SUVs make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. In Jaipur you ascend Amber Fort by private vehicle rather than by elephant, and in Ranthambore our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is October to March, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

Golden Triangle & Tiger Safari Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    A VIP representative meets you as you leave the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur then drives you to The Oberoi New Delhi, about 45 minutes away, where the rooms look out over the Delhi Golf Club. The rest of the day is yours to settle in after your flight.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi

    Your private historian begins in Old Delhi, the walled city of the Mughal emperor Shah Jahan. A cycle-rickshaw weaves through the lanes of Chandni Chowk and the spice market of Khari Baoli to the great Jama Masjid. After lunch, visit Humayun's Tomb, the garden tomb that inspired the Taj Mahal, and the Qutub Minar, a soaring tower of carved red sandstone, before driving past India Gate and the avenues of Lutyens' Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  3. Day 3: New Delhi to Agra

    After breakfast, drive to Agra on the Yamuna Expressway, about three and a half hours. Check in to The Oberoi Amarvilas, just 600 metres from the Taj Mahal, where every room looks out to the monument. In the late afternoon, cross the Yamuna to Mehtab Bagh, the moonlight garden, to watch the sun set behind the Taj from the far bank of the river.

    Overnight: The Oberoi Amarvilas, Agra

  4. Day 4: Agra to Jaipur

    Rise before dawn for the short ride by private buggy to the Taj Mahal, timed to see the marble turn from grey to gold with the first light. Return for breakfast, then explore Agra Fort, the red-sandstone citadel of the Mughal emperors. Later, drive to Jaipur, about five hours, pausing at Fatehpur Sikri, the abandoned capital of the emperor Akbar. You arrive at Rambagh Palace in time for dinner.

    Overnight: Rambagh Palace, Jaipur

  5. Day 5: Jaipur

    A private historian guides you through the rose-pink city. The morning begins at Amber Fort, which you ascend by private vehicle, and continues through the courtyards of the City Palace and the giant astronomical instruments of Jantar Mantar, with a pause before the honeycomb façade of the Hawa Mahal. The afternoon is yours to shape from the leisure menu, perhaps a vintage car drive or a block printing session with master artisans.

    Overnight: Rambagh Palace, Jaipur

  6. Day 6: Jaipur to Ranthambore National Park

    A drive of three and a half to four hours through the Rajasthan countryside brings you to Aman-i-Khas in time for lunch. Each of its ten tents is attended by a personal butler and opens onto a private sun deck. In the afternoon, set out on your first safari, through dry, open forest where tigers are often seen in full daylight.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  7. Day 7: Ranthambore National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore Ranthambore's zones, the most celebrated winding past the lakes of Padam Talao and Rajbagh, where sambar wade among the water lilies and mugger crocodiles bask on the banks. Your naturalist shares the lineage of the tigers who hunt here, many descended from the legendary tigress Machli. Between drives, unwind by the camp's stepwell pool or in the spa tent.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  8. Day 8: Ranthambore National Park

    After a dawn safari, climb to Ranthambore Fort, part of a UNESCO World Heritage Site, with the ancient Trinetra Ganesh temple within its walls and sweeping views across the reserve. A final afternoon safari is followed by a farewell dinner around the campfire beneath the stars.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  9. Day 9: Ranthambore to New Delhi

    After breakfast, your chauffeur drives you back to the capital on the Delhi–Mumbai Expressway, about six hours. Your final evening is at The Oberoi New Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  10. Day 10: Departure

    Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

A note from our planners

The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays, so we arrange your days around it. Ranthambore is open from October to June, and Aman-i-Khas welcomes guests from October to May. October to March brings the most comfortable weather for the cities.

Every property on this journey has been handpicked by our team. They are lodges and hotels we have worked with and returned to over many years, chosen for their setting, the quality of their naturalists, the warmth of their service and their care for the forests and communities around them. We recommend only places we know intimately, so you can rest assured that each stay will be as exceptional as the wilderness beyond its doors. If you have something particular in mind, our safari specialists will gladly find the perfect stay for your needs, including properties beyond those listed here.

  • New Delhi – The Oberoi New Delhi (Days 1, 2 & 9): A landmark of Indian hospitality, with polished service and views across the Delhi Golf Club.

  • Agra – The Oberoi Amarvilas (Day 3): Just 600 metres from the Taj Mahal, with views of the monument from every room, set among terraced lawns, reflection pools and pavilions inspired by Mughal palaces.

  • Ranthambore – Aman-i-Khas (Days 6–8): Ten luxury tents on the fringes of the reserve, each with a private sun deck and personal butler, with a stepwell pool and spa tent.

Ranthambore

  • The Oberoi Vanyavilas: Luxury tents with rich Rajasthani interiors, set among landscaped gardens and fountains.
  • Sher Bagh (Sujan): A classic canvas camp that evokes the grand safari camps of the 1920s, with a strong conservation ethos.
  • Sawai Madhopur Lodge – IHCL SeleQtions: A 1936 royal hunting lodge of the Jaipur royal family, full of Art Deco character.

Popular Fauna

  • Bengal Tiger
  • Indian Leopard
  • Sloth Bear
  • Striped Hyena
  • Sambar, Chital and Nilgai
  • Mugger Crocodile
  • Birdlife, including the Indian Paradise Flycatcher, Crested Serpent Eagle and Painted Stork

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Ranthambore National Park: Where the Aravalli and Vindhya hills meet, Ranthambore's dry forest of dhok trees, banyans and lakes is scattered with the ruins of a royal past. Its tigers are famously at ease in daylight, making it one of the finest reserves in the world for tiger photography.

UNESCO World Heritage Sites

  • Humayun's Tomb and the Qutub Minar, Delhi: A 16th-century garden tomb and a soaring 12th-century tower of carved sandstone.
  • Taj Mahal and Agra Fort: Shah Jahan's marble memorial to Mumtaz Mahal, and the red-sandstone citadel of the Mughal emperors.
  • Fatehpur Sikri: The capital Akbar built and abandoned within a generation, preserved almost intact.
  • Amber Fort and Ranthambore Fort: Two of the Hill Forts of Rajasthan, one above Maota Lake and one rising from the heart of the tiger reserve.
  • Jantar Mantar and the Walled City of Jaipur: An 18th-century observatory of giant stone instruments, within Maharaja Sawai Jai Singh II's planned, rose-pink city.

Golden Triangle Tour with Tiger Safari FAQs

How many days do I need for the Golden Triangle and a tiger safari?

About nine or ten nights lets you see Delhi, Agra and Jaipur at an unhurried pace and spend three nights in Ranthambore, enough for several safaris and a good chance of seeing tigers.

When is the best time for a Golden Triangle tour with tiger safari?

October to March brings warm days and cool evenings, ideal for the cities. Ranthambore is open from October to June, and the warmer months from March onwards are often the most rewarding for tiger sightings.

What are my chances of seeing a tiger in Ranthambore?

Tigers are wild, so sightings can never be guaranteed. Ranthambore's tigers are famously at ease in daylight, however, and three nights with expert naturalists give you an excellent chance.

Is the Taj Mahal open every day?

The Taj Mahal is closed on Fridays, so we plan your sunrise visit around it.

How do I travel between the cities and Ranthambore?

Every journey is made in a comfortable, chauffeur-driven private SUV. Delhi to Agra takes about three and a half hours, Agra to Jaipur about five, Jaipur to Ranthambore about four, and Ranthambore back to Delhi about six on the expressway.
