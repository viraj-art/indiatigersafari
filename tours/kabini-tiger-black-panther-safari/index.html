About This Kabini Safari

This Kabini safari takes you to the southern edge of Nagarhole National Park in Karnataka, where teak forest meets the wide backwaters of the Kabini River. Kabini is one of the finest places in Asia to see wild elephants, and its tigers, leopards and packs of dhole are seen often. It is also famed for its "black panther", which is not a separate species but an Indian leopard with melanism, a rare genetic condition that produces excess dark pigment in its skin and coat. Its appearances have drawn wildlife photographers from around the world.

You stay at Kabini River Lodge, once the hunting lodge of the Maharajas of Mysore, with jeep safaris into Nagarhole and boat safaris on the backwaters, where elephant herds gather to graze along the shore. The journey then crosses into Kerala for two nights at Fringe Ford, a secluded private reserve on the edge of Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary, where you explore the forest on foot in search of the Malabar trogon, hornbills and the Nilgiri langur. It begins at Taj West End, Bengaluru's oldest hotel, set in 20 acres of gardens. A VIP welcome at Bengaluru airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and a short domestic flight make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Our partner lodges employ and train people from neighbouring villages, our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting, and your park fees help protect the reserves themselves. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. Kabini can be visited for most of the year, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

Kabini & Wayanad Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in Bengaluru

    A VIP representative meets you as you leave the aircraft at Kempegowda International Airport and guides you through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur drives you to Taj West End, about an hour away. Opened as a ten-bed boarding house in 1887, it is the city's oldest hotel, its rooms and suites set among 20 acres of lawns and old trees. Spend the day at rest by the pool or wander the gardens, where birds are surprisingly plentiful for the heart of a city.

    Overnight: Taj West End, Bengaluru

  2. Day 2: Bengaluru to Kabini

    After breakfast, drive south-west for four to five hours, past the royal city of Mysuru and into the farmland and forest of the Kabini valley. Kabini River Lodge, once the hunting lodge of the Maharajas of Mysore, stands in wide riverside grounds on the southern edge of Nagarhole National Park. After lunch, set out on your first safari, by jeep into the forest or by boat on the backwaters.

    Overnight: Kabini River Lodge, Kabini

  3. Day 3: Nagarhole National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore Nagarhole's teak and rosewood forest, bamboo thickets and open swamps. Chital, sambar and gaur are plentiful, and your naturalist listens for the alarm calls that betray a tiger or leopard on the move. Dhole hunt in packs here, and Kabini is one of the very few places on earth where a so-called black panther has been seen and photographed in the wild. It is not a separate species but a leopard with melanism, a rare genetic condition that darkens its skin and coat, and in the right light its rosettes still show through.

    Overnight: Kabini River Lodge, Kabini

  4. Day 4: Nagarhole National Park

    After a dawn jeep safari, spend the afternoon on the water. A boat safari on the Kabini backwaters brings you close to elephant herds grazing along the shore, with mugger crocodiles, smooth-coated otters and islands crowded with river terns. Ospreys and grey-headed fish eagles perch in the drowned trees. In the evening, gather on the lawns beneath the lodge's great trees for dinner.

    Overnight: Kabini River Lodge, Kabini

  5. Day 5: Kabini to Wayanad

    Enjoy a final morning safari in Nagarhole and a leisurely breakfast, then drive about three hours west into the hills of Kerala. Fringe Ford is a former cardamom plantation in a secluded valley, now a private reserve of around 400 hectares that shares its boundaries with Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary and the Tholpetty forest. Its eight rooms lie in a historic bungalow and beside a perennial stream. In the late afternoon, take your first walk in the forest with your naturalist, then gather around a bonfire by the stream.

    Overnight: Fringe Ford, Wayanad

  6. Day 6: Fringe Ford, Wayanad

    Today is spent on foot. Trails lead through the forest, along streams and up to a mountain ridge, and your naturalist chooses those that best suit your pace. Watch for Nilgiri langurs and Malabar giant squirrels in the canopy, and for endemic birds such as the Malabar trogon, Malabar grey hornbill and vernal hanging parrot. Elephants and gaur also move through these forests, and your naturalist may reveal them from a respectful distance.

    Overnight: Fringe Ford, Wayanad

  7. Day 7: Departure

    After a final early walk in search of the Malabar trogon, your chauffeur drives you to Kannur, two and a half to three hours away, for your flight to Bengaluru and your onward journey.

A note from our planners

Nagarhole's safaris follow routes and timings set by the forest department, and at least one of your safaris will be by boat on the Kabini backwaters, as park rules require. Safaris from Kabini River Lodge run in the lodge's own vehicles with its naturalists, and we request a jeep for your party alone wherever permits allow.

Birds of Kabini and Wayanad: Species Checklist

Nagarhole has more than 250 recorded bird species, and the forests of Wayanad add many found only in the Western Ghats. These are among the birds our naturalists see most often; sightings vary with the season.

  • Raptors and owls: Grey-headed Fish Eagle, Lesser Fish Eagle, Crested Serpent Eagle, Changeable Hawk-Eagle, Oriental Honey Buzzard, Osprey, Red-headed Vulture, Shikra, Brown Fish Owl, Jungle Owlet
  • On the Kabini backwaters: River Tern, Small Pratincole, Oriental Darter, Great Cormorant, Indian Cormorant, Woolly-necked Stork, Black-headed Ibis, Grey Heron, Purple Heron, Stork-billed Kingfisher, Pied Kingfisher, White-throated Kingfisher
  • Forest birds of Nagarhole: Malabar Pied Hornbill, Indian Peafowl, Grey Junglefowl, Red Spurfowl, White-bellied Woodpecker, Black-rumped Flameback, Coppersmith Barbet, White-cheeked Barbet, Chestnut-headed Bee-eater, Blue-bearded Bee-eater, Indian Pitta, Indian Paradise Flycatcher, Black-naped Monarch, Greater Racket-tailed Drongo, Rufous Treepie, Plum-headed Parakeet, Yellow-footed Green Pigeon, Green Imperial Pigeon, Golden-fronted Leafbird, Black-hooded Oriole
  • Western Ghats specialities at Fringe Ford: Great Hornbill, Malabar Grey Hornbill, Malabar Trogon, Malabar Parakeet, Vernal Hanging Parrot, Mountain Imperial Pigeon, Malabar Whistling Thrush, White-bellied Treepie, Southern Hill Myna, Asian Fairy-bluebird, Crimson-backed Sunbird, Orange-headed Thrush, Grey-headed Bulbul, Flame-throated Bulbul, Yellow-browed Bulbul, Indian Scimitar Babbler, Rufous Babbler

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Nagarhole National Park: Covering about 643 sq km of Karnataka's Kodagu and Mysuru districts, Nagarhole takes its name from the snake-like streams that wind through its forests. Declared a tiger reserve in 1999, it is part of the Nilgiri Biosphere Reserve, and the Kabini reservoir separates it from neighbouring Bandipur.
  • Wayanad Wildlife Sanctuary: Set in the hills of northern Kerala, Wayanad joins Nagarhole, Bandipur and Mudumalai to form one of the largest continuous forest landscapes in southern India. Its moist forests shelter elephants, tigers and gaur alongside many species found only in the Western Ghats.

Kabini Tiger & Black Panther Safari FAQs

Can I see the black panther in Kabini?

Kabini's "black panther" is not a separate species but an Indian leopard with melanism, a rare genetic condition that darkens its skin and coat; look closely and its rosettes still show. It is one of the rarest sights in Indian wildlife, and a sighting can never be promised. Kabini is one of the very few places in the world where one has been seen and photographed in the wild, and three nights of safaris give you a genuine chance, alongside excellent odds of elephants, leopards and dhole.

When is the best time for a Kabini safari?

Kabini can be visited for most of the year. October to February brings green forest and pleasant weather, while March to June, when the backwaters recede, is the classic season for elephant herds and predator sightings.

How do I get to Kabini?

Kabini lies about 210 km from Bengaluru, a drive of four to five hours with your chauffeur. This itinerary begins with a night in Bengaluru to rest after your international flight and ends with a short flight from Kannur back to Bengaluru.

Is this Kabini safari suitable for families?

Yes. Jeep and boat safaris suit most ages, and the forest walks at Fringe Ford are paced to your group. Our safari specialists will advise on the best rooms and activities for younger travellers as we plan your journey.

How do I book a private Kabini safari?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will tailor this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private, bespoke and curated for you, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead, especially for the popular months from March to June.
