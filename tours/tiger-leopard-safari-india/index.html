About This Tiger & Leopard Safari

This tiger and leopard safari in India explores two contrasting reserves in the hills of Madhya Pradesh. Pench, the teak country of Kipling's The Jungle Book, is one of Central India's most rewarding tiger forests. Satpura, a wilderness of sandstone peaks, deep gorges and river backwaters, is known for its leopards and sloth bears, and is one of the few tiger reserves in India you can explore on foot and by canoe.

This is an active and intimate journey. Alongside private 4x4 jeep safaris with expert naturalists, you cycle forest trails on the Kanha–Pench tiger corridor, walk through Satpura's forests with a naturalist and forest guide, glide along the Denwa River by canoe and spend a night under canvas at a private fly camp. You stay at two Pugdundee Safaris lodges, Pench Tree Lodge and Denwa Backwater Escape, both celebrated for their naturalists and their close ties to the forest communities around them. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and domestic flights make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Our partner lodges employ and train people from neighbouring villages, our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting, and your park fees help protect the reserves themselves. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The parks are open from October to June, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

What You Will Experience

  • Tigers and leopards in two great reserves, Pench and Satpura, in the forests that inspired The Jungle Book.
  • Private 4x4 jeep safaris at dawn and dusk, reserved for your party alone and led by expert naturalists.
  • Cycling on the Kanha–Pench tiger corridor, with lunch at Sakata, a colonial forest rest house built in 1904.

Pench & Satpura Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    A VIP representative meets you at the aircraft door and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur then drives you to The Oberoi New Delhi, where the afternoon is yours to rest, swim or enjoy a treatment at the spa. Dine quietly at the hotel ahead of tomorrow's flight into the heart of India.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi to Pench National Park

    A morning flight carries you south to Nagpur, where your chauffeur is waiting for the drive of three to four hours to Pench. Pench Tree Lodge stands on 40 acres of private forest near the quiet Karmajhiri gate, its tree houses raised among the branches. Settle onto your deck, then take a gentle walk through the lodge's forest with your naturalist as the evening light fades.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  3. Day 3: Pench National Park

    Spend the day on morning and afternoon safaris from the Karmajhiri gate, where far fewer vehicles enter than at the park's busier Turia gate. Midway through the morning safari, stop at a designated spot in the forest for a breakfast hamper from the lodge kitchen. Teak forest opens onto grassy meadows along the Pench River, and your naturalist reads the alarm calls of chital and langur for signs of a tiger or leopard on the move. Packs of dhole and herds of gaur are often seen too. Dinner is served beside the lodge's organic garden, cooked in clay ovens and over an open hearth.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  4. Day 4: Cycling on the Kanha–Pench Corridor

    After breakfast, drive about 30 minutes to Rukhad, a buffer-zone sanctuary on the tiger corridor that links Pench with Kanha. With your naturalist and a local forest guide, cycle quiet trails through teak and bamboo at a gentle pace, with a support vehicle never far behind, and learn how these forest links allow tigers to move between reserves. Lunch is served at Sakata, a colonial forest rest house built in 1904. Back at the lodge, spend the late afternoon in its underground photography hide as birds and animals come to drink.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  5. Day 5: Pench National Park

    A final day of safaris in Pench, perhaps exploring the forest along the Pench River, where leopards hunt among the rocks and thickets, with breakfast in the forest midway through the morning safari. Your naturalist shares the story of Collarwali, the Pench tigress celebrated for raising 29 cubs, and the generations of tigers she left behind. Return to the lodge for a last evening among the trees.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  6. Day 6: Pench to Satpura National Park

    After breakfast, drive west for five to six hours through the farmland and forested hills of Madhya Pradesh, with a picnic lunch along the way. Denwa Backwater Escape sits on the backwaters of the Denwa River, looking across open grassland to the Pachmarhi hills. Arrive in time to watch the sun set over the water from your veranda.

    Overnight: Denwa Backwater Escape (Pugdundee Safaris), Satpura

  7. Day 7: Satpura National Park

    The morning begins with a short boat crossing of the Denwa to the Madhai gate, where your jeep waits for a safari into the park. Satpura's sandstone ravines, teak forest and meadows shelter leopards, sloth bears, gaur and the striking Indian giant squirrel, as well as tigers. In the afternoon, set out by canoe along the backwaters, where mugger crocodiles bask on the banks and ospreys, river terns and woolly-necked storks hunt along the shore.

    Overnight: Denwa Backwater Escape (Pugdundee Safaris), Satpura

  8. Day 8: Walking Safari and Fly Camp

    Satpura is one of the few tiger reserves in India where you may explore the forest on foot. This morning, cross the river by boat and walk with your naturalist and a forest guide, reading tracks, studying birds and plants, and feeling the forest around you in a way no vehicle allows. After lunch, transfer to your private fly camp, where spacious walk-in tents, a dining tent and a dedicated camp team await. Take a short walk near camp as the light softens, then dine by lantern light beneath the stars.

    Overnight: Private Fly Camp, Satpura

  9. Day 9: Satpura National Park

    Wake to birdsong and a dawn walk from camp, then return to the lodge for a late breakfast. In the afternoon, a final jeep safari offers another chance of leopard and sloth bear. Your last evening is celebrated with a private dinner on the riverbank.

    Overnight: Denwa Backwater Escape (Pugdundee Safaris), Satpura

  10. Day 10: Satpura to New Delhi

    Spend a final early morning on the water, perhaps kayaking across the still backwaters. After breakfast, your chauffeur drives you to Bhopal, three and a half to four hours away, for your flight to Delhi. Your final evening is at The Oberoi New Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  11. Day 11: Departure

    Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

A note from our planners

The Madhya Pradesh reserves rest on Wednesday afternoons and close for Holi and Diwali. Walking safaris, canoe safaris and the fly camp depend on forest department permits and water levels. Your itinerary is arranged around these, and an in-lodge experience takes the place of any affected activity.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Pench National Park: Named for the river that divides it, Pench spreads across the hills of Seoni and Chhindwara in a mosaic of teak forest, bamboo and open meadow. Its core of 411 sq km supports tigers, leopards and dhole, and the surrounding landscape gave Kipling the setting for Mowgli's adventures.
  • Satpura National Park: Satpura covers 524 sq km of sandstone peaks, gorges and river backwaters, and together with the Bori and Pachmarhi sanctuaries forms the Satpura Tiger Reserve. The reserve is on UNESCO's Tentative List for World Heritage status and is one of the few in India that welcomes visitors on foot, by canoe and by boat.

Tiger & Leopard Safari FAQs

What are my chances of seeing a tiger and a leopard?

Wild animals can never be guaranteed, but this route gives you excellent odds. Pench is one of Central India's most rewarding tiger reserves, Satpura is known for its leopards and sloth bears, and expert naturalists accompany you throughout.

When is the best time for a tiger and leopard safari in India?

The Madhya Pradesh reserves are open from October to June. November to March brings cool, comfortable days, while April to June is often the most rewarding time for sightings, as wildlife gathers near water.

What is a walking safari in Satpura like?

You walk in a small group with your naturalist and a forest guide, usually in the cool of the early morning. The pace is gentle, with time to study tracks, birds and plants, and every walk follows the forest department's rules for that zone.

Is this tiger and leopard safari suitable for families?

Yes, for active families who enjoy the outdoors. The cycling is gentle, with a support vehicle on hand, and our safari specialists can adapt walks and activities for younger travellers. Some activities carry minimum age limits set by the forest department, and we will advise on these as we plan your journey.

How do I book a private tiger and leopard safari?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will tailor this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private, bespoke and curated for you, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead for the best choice of lodges and park permits.
