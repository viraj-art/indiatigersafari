About This Luxury Tiger Safari

India has long been ruled by two kinds of sovereign: the royal houses of Rajasthan and the tigers of its forests. This luxury tiger safari holiday brings both together. It begins among the palaces of Jaipur and the fort-crowned lakes of Ranthambore, then travels deep into the sal and teak forests of Central India, where Bandhavgarh, Kanha and Pench reveal the Bengal tiger in its most majestic setting.

You set out at dawn and again in the late afternoon in a private 4x4 jeep, reserved for your party alone, with naturalists who know each reserve and its resident tigers intimately. Between safaris, you return to India's finest wildlife addresses, from Aman-i-Khas in Ranthambore to Mahua Kothi, Banjaar Tola and Pench Tree Lodge in Central India, and in Jaipur you stay at Rambagh Palace, once the home of the Maharaja. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and domestic flights make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Our partner lodges employ and train people from neighbouring villages, our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting, and in Jaipur you ascend Amber Fort by private vehicle rather than by elephant. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is November to April, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

Luxury Tiger Safari Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    Your journey begins the moment you land. A VIP representative meets you as you leave the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur then drives you in a comfortable private SUV to The Oberoi New Delhi, about 45 minutes away, where the rooms look out over the greens of the Delhi Golf Club. The rest of the day is yours to rest after your flight, or to dine at one of the hotel's acclaimed restaurants.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi to Jaipur

    After breakfast, your chauffeur drives you to Jaipur, the rose-pink capital of Rajasthan, about five hours away. You arrive for a late lunch at Rambagh Palace, once the home of the Maharaja of Jaipur. As evening falls, stroll through its Mughal gardens while peacocks wander across the lawns, then dine in the palace's restaurants.

    Overnight: Rambagh Palace, Jaipur

  3. Day 3: Jaipur

    A private historian guides you through the city's great monuments. The morning begins at Amber Fort, which you ascend by private vehicle, and continues through the courtyards of the City Palace, with a pause before the honeycomb façade of the Hawa Mahal. The afternoon is yours to shape from the leisure menu: perhaps a vintage car drive through Jaipur's historic estates, a block printing session with master artisans, or a private evening of folk music and puppetry.

    Overnight: Rambagh Palace, Jaipur

  4. Day 4: Jaipur to Ranthambore National Park

    A drive of about three and a half hours through the Rajasthan countryside brings you to Aman-i-Khas in time for lunch. This intimate camp of spacious tents sits on the edge of the reserve. In the afternoon you set out on your first safari, through dry, open forest where tigers are often seen in full daylight.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  5. Day 5: Ranthambore National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore Ranthambore's zones. The most celebrated wind past the lakes of Padam Talao and Rajbagh, where sambar wade among the water lilies and mugger crocodiles bask on the banks. Ruined pavilions and watchtowers rise from the forest, and your naturalist shares the lineage of the tigers who hunt around them, many descended from the legendary tigress Machli.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  6. Day 6: Ranthambore National Park

    After a dawn safari, climb to Ranthambore Fort, a UNESCO World Heritage Site high above the forest, with the ancient Trinetra Ganesh temple within its walls. The afternoon brings a final safari, or time at leisure in camp.

    Overnight: Aman-i-Khas, Ranthambore

  7. Day 7: Ranthambore to New Delhi

    After breakfast, your chauffeur drives you back to the capital on the Delhi–Mumbai Expressway, a journey of about six hours. Tonight you stay at Roseate House in Aerocity, minutes from the airport, so tomorrow's morning flight begins without a moment's rush.

    Overnight: Roseate House, New Delhi

  8. Day 8: New Delhi to Bandhavgarh National Park

    A short morning flight takes you to Jabalpur, where your chauffeur is waiting for the four-hour drive into the hills of Bandhavgarh. You arrive at Mahua Kothi, a Taj Safaris lodge whose private cottages are modelled on Central Indian village homes and set among mahua trees. The afternoon is yours for tea on your veranda and the first evening calls of the forest.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  9. Day 9: Bandhavgarh National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore the reserve's zones. In Tala, the tracks wind beneath the sandstone cliffs of the ancient hilltop fort and past Shesh Shaiya, a reclining stone Vishnu. The open meadows and bamboo groves here are among the most rewarding tiger habitats in India.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  10. Day 10: Bandhavgarh National Park

    Another day of safaris, with the chance of tigresses with cubs, leopards and sloth bears. For dedicated wildlife lovers, a full-day permit allows uninterrupted time in the forest from dawn to dusk, subject to availability.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  11. Day 11: Bandhavgarh to Kanha National Park

    After an optional early safari and breakfast, a five-to-six-hour drive through rural Madhya Pradesh brings you to Banjaar Tola. Its tented suites line the banks of the Banjar River and look across to the forests of Kanha. Settle onto your private deck as the evening draws in.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  12. Day 12: Kanha National Park

    Dawn mist rises from Kanha's great meadows, where herds of hard-ground barasingha graze and packs of dhole hunt through the long grass. The heat of the day is for lunch and rest at the lodge. In the afternoon, your safari can end at Bamni Dadar, a plateau where gaur and sambar browse against the setting sun.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  13. Day 13: Kanha National Park

    After a morning safari, spend the afternoon on a guided walk to a neighbouring Baiga or Gond village, at a Gond art workshop, or relaxing on your deck above the river.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  14. Day 14: Kanha to Pench National Park

    A drive of four to five hours west brings you to Pench Tree Lodge, set on 40 acres of private forest near the quiet Karmajhiri gate of Pench, its tree houses raised among the branches. You arrive by mid-afternoon, with the evening free to settle in.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  15. Day 15: Pench National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris explore the teak forests of the Seoni hills, the country Kipling chose as the setting for The Jungle Book. Midway through the morning safari, pause for a breakfast hamper at a designated spot in the forest. Plentiful deer support tigers, leopards and dhole. That evening, a private farewell dinner is served by lantern light, with your naturalist sharing the season's stories.

    Overnight: Pench Tree Lodge (Pugdundee Safaris), Pench

  16. Day 16: Pench to New Delhi

    Take a final dawn safari if you wish, then drive to Nagpur, about three to four hours away, for your flight to Delhi. Your farewell evening is at The Oberoi New Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  17. Day 17: Departure

    Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Ranthambore National Park: Ranthambore lies where the Aravalli and Vindhya hills meet. Its dry deciduous forest of dhok trees, banyans and lakes is scattered with the ruins of a royal past. Its tigers are famously at ease in daylight, making it one of the finest reserves in the world for tiger photography.
  • Bandhavgarh National Park: Bandhavgarh is a compact, hilly reserve of sal forest, bamboo and grassy valleys, overlooked by an ancient fort linked in legend to the Ramayana. It has one of the highest tiger densities in India, and its tigers have been celebrated by wildlife filmmakers for decades.
  • Kanha National Park: Kanha is one of Asia's great protected landscapes. Towering sal forest opens onto wide grassy meadows in the Maikal hills. It is renowned for bringing the hard-ground barasingha back from the brink, and for its excellent sightings of tigers, dhole and gaur.
  • Pench National Park: Pench is named for the river that runs through it. Its forests of teak and mixed woodland in the Seoni hills are the setting of Kipling's The Jungle Book. Plentiful prey supports healthy populations of tigers, leopards and dhole, as well as rich birdlife along the river.

Luxury Tiger Safari FAQs

Which national parks does this luxury tiger safari visit?

Ranthambore in Rajasthan and Bandhavgarh, Kanha and Pench in Central India, four of the finest tiger reserves in the country, with a royal stay in Jaipur on the way.

When is the best time for a tiger safari in India?

November to April offers pleasant weather and is when the seasonal camps in Ranthambore are open. The parks stay open until the end of June, and the hotter months often bring excellent tiger sightings.

What are my chances of seeing a tiger?

Tigers are wild, so sightings can never be guaranteed. With several days in each of four reserves, including Bandhavgarh and Ranthambore, which are famous for their tigers, your chances are excellent.

Can I shorten the journey or change the parks?

Yes. Every departure is private, so our safari specialists can shorten the route, swap parks or add extra days in the field to suit your dates and interests.

How far in advance should I book?

Safari permits are limited and the finest lodges fill quickly for peak dates, so we recommend booking four to six months ahead.
