About This Keoladeo Birding Tour

This Keoladeo bird sanctuary tour adds two days at one of the world's great wetlands to your journey through northern India. Keoladeo National Park at Bharatpur, a UNESCO World Heritage Site, was once the duck-shooting reserve of the Maharajas of Bharatpur. Today its shallow lakes, marshes and woodland are a haven for more than 350 species of birds, from towering sarus cranes and nesting painted storks to great flocks of ducks and geese that arrive each winter from Central Asia and beyond.

You stay at Laxmi Vilas Palace, a former royal residence of the Jat rulers of Bharatpur, still home to the family, with gilded halls, courtyards and a pool set in wide lawns, just 2 km from the sanctuary. With an expert birding naturalist, you explore the park's raised paths on foot, by bicycle or in a cycle rickshaw driven by a local guide trained in birdwatching. Bharatpur lies only an hour from Agra, so the extension fits easily before or after the Taj Mahal, Jaipur or Ranthambore, with chauffeur-driven private SUVs making every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Keoladeo's licensed rickshaw guides earn their living from the park's birds, and your park fees help protect this fragile wetland. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is October to March, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

Keoladeo Birding Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in Bharatpur

    Your chauffeur collects you in Agra, Jaipur or Delhi and drives you to Bharatpur, about an hour from Agra. Laxmi Vilas Palace welcomes you with its courtyards, gilded halls and lawns. In the late afternoon, your birding naturalist takes you into Keoladeo for a first look at the wetlands as the light turns gold and the cranes call across the water.

    Overnight: Laxmi Vilas Palace, Bharatpur

  2. Day 2: Keoladeo National Park

    A full day in the park. Set out at dawn along the raised paths that divide the wetlands, on foot, by bicycle or in a cycle rickshaw with a local guide. Sarus cranes stride through the shallows, painted storks and spoonbills feed in the marshes, and in winter bar-headed geese, pelicans and many kinds of duck crowd the open water. In the woodland, look for dusky eagle-owls, spotted owlets and the Indian rock python at Python Point. Return to the palace for a quiet afternoon by the pool before a final drive at dusk.

    Overnight: Laxmi Vilas Palace, Bharatpur

  3. Day 3: Departure from Bharatpur

    Enjoy a last early walk in the park for any species still on your list, then continue to Agra, Jaipur, Ranthambore or Delhi.

A note from our planners

Keoladeo's wetlands depend on the monsoon, and water levels vary from year to year. Bicycles and cycle rickshaws are the main way to explore, as motor vehicles are not allowed beyond the entrance, and we arrange the best guides in advance.

Birds of Keoladeo National Park: Species Checklist

More than 350 bird species have been recorded at Keoladeo. These are among those our naturalists see most often, with the wetlands at their richest from October to March.

  • Cranes, storks and ibises: Sarus Crane, Common Crane, Painted Stork, Black-necked Stork, Woolly-necked Stork, Asian Openbill, Eurasian Spoonbill, Black-headed Ibis, Glossy Ibis, Red-naped Ibis
  • Pelicans, cormorants and herons: Great White Pelican, Oriental Darter, Great Cormorant, Indian Cormorant, Little Cormorant, Grey Heron, Purple Heron, Black-crowned Night Heron, Indian Pond Heron, Great Egret, Intermediate Egret, Little Egret, Cattle Egret, Yellow Bittern
  • Ducks and geese: Bar-headed Goose, Greylag Goose, Knob-billed Duck, Lesser Whistling Duck, Cotton Pygmy Goose, Ruddy Shelduck, Indian Spot-billed Duck, Gadwall, Eurasian Wigeon, Northern Shoveler, Northern Pintail, Common Teal, Garganey, Common Pochard, Ferruginous Duck, Tufted Duck
  • Rails, jacanas and waders: Grey-headed Swamphen, Common Moorhen, White-breasted Waterhen, Bronze-winged Jacana, Pheasant-tailed Jacana, Black-winged Stilt, Red-wattled Lapwing, White-tailed Lapwing, Common Snipe, Wood Sandpiper, Green Sandpiper, Common Sandpiper
  • Raptors: Greater Spotted Eagle, Indian Spotted Eagle, Eastern Imperial Eagle, Booted Eagle, Western Marsh Harrier, Osprey, Black-winged Kite, Shikra, Crested Serpent Eagle
  • Owls: Dusky Eagle-Owl, Spotted Owlet, Indian Scops Owl, Collared Scops Owl
  • Kingfishers and bee-eaters: Pied Kingfisher, White-throated Kingfisher, Common Kingfisher, Green Bee-eater
  • Woodland and scrub birds: Indian Peafowl, Black Francolin, Grey Francolin, Indian Grey Hornbill, Coppersmith Barbet, Brown-headed Barbet, Black-rumped Flameback, Rose-ringed Parakeet, Yellow-footed Green Pigeon, Greater Coucal, Asian Koel, Indian Roller
  • Winter visitors among the reeds and trees: Bluethroat, Black Redstart, Siberian Stonechat, Red-breasted Flycatcher, Citrine Wagtail, Western Yellow Wagtail, Common Chiffchaff

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

Keoladeo Birding Extension FAQs

When is the best time to visit Keoladeo National Park?

October to March is best, when migrant waterfowl, cranes and pelicans fill the wetlands. From July to September, the park's heronries come alive with nesting painted storks, egrets and herons.

What birds will I see at Keoladeo?

Sarus cranes, painted storks, spoonbills, pelicans, owls and many species of duck and geese are seen on most winter visits. More than 350 species have been recorded here, so even experienced birders find something new, though wild birds can never be guaranteed.

How do I get to Bharatpur?

Bharatpur is about an hour by road from Agra and around four hours from Jaipur or Delhi. It fits easily between the Taj Mahal and Jaipur or Ranthambore on any journey through northern India.

Is this birding extension suitable for families and beginners?

Yes. The paths are flat, the cycle rickshaw rides are gentle, and our naturalists tailor each outing to your interest, whether you are a keen birder or simply enjoy watching wildlife.
