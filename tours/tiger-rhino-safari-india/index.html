About This Tiger & Rhino Safari

This tiger and rhino safari in India brings together two of the country's most celebrated wildlife landscapes. In Central India, the sal forests and meadows of Bandhavgarh and Kanha offer some of the finest tiger viewing anywhere. In Assam, the floodplain grasslands of Kaziranga, a UNESCO World Heritage Site on the Brahmaputra, hold the world's largest population of the greater one-horned rhinoceros, alongside wild elephants, water buffalo and a remarkable density of tigers.

You spend seven nights in Central India, at the Taj Safaris lodges of Mahua Kothi in Bandhavgarh and Banjaar Tola in Kanha, with time for repeated morning and afternoon safaris. The journey then flies east to Kaziranga for three nights at Diphlu River Lodge, an intimate retreat of twelve cottages raised on stilts beside the Diphlu River. Every safari is taken in a private 4x4 jeep, reserved for your party alone, with naturalists who know each forest intimately. A VIP welcome at Delhi airport, chauffeur-driven private SUVs and domestic flights make every transfer effortless.

This is travel that gives back. Our partner lodges employ and train people from neighbouring villages, our naturalists follow the forest department's code of conduct at every sighting, and your park fees help protect the reserves themselves. Your journey also contributes to TOFTigers, the wildlife tourism and conservation alliance that won a Travel + Leisure Global Vision Award in 2025. The best time to travel is November to April, when both regions are open, and every departure is private and bespoke, curated around your dates and interests.

What You Will Experience

  • Tigers and one-horned rhinos in one journey, from the forests of Central India to the grasslands of Assam.
  • Private 4x4 jeep safaris at dawn and dusk, reserved for your party alone and led by expert naturalists.
  • Bandhavgarh's ancient hills, crowned by a 2,000-year-old fort and famed for one of India's highest tiger densities.
  • Four nights in Kanha, where the rare hard-ground barasingha grazes on the great meadows.
  • Kaziranga National Park, home to the world's largest population of the greater one-horned rhinoceros.
  • Wild elephants, water buffalo and swamp deer on the floodplain of the Brahmaputra.
  • Taj Safaris lodges and Diphlu River Lodge, three of the finest wildlife retreats in India.

Bandhavgarh, Kanha & Kaziranga Itinerary: Day by Day

  1. Day 1: Arrival in New Delhi

    A VIP representative meets you at the aircraft and guides you swiftly through immigration, baggage reclaim and customs. Your chauffeur drives you to The Oberoi New Delhi for a restful first evening before tomorrow's flight into Central India.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  2. Day 2: New Delhi to Bandhavgarh National Park

    A morning flight takes you to Jabalpur, where your chauffeur is waiting for the four-hour drive to Bandhavgarh. Mahua Kothi, a Taj Safaris lodge, welcomes you with private cottages modelled on the village homes of Central India and set in a grove of mahua trees. Spend the late afternoon on your veranda as the forest settles towards evening.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  3. Day 3: Bandhavgarh National Park

    Morning and afternoon safaris take you into the reserve's zones, among them Tala, Magadhi and Khitauli. Bandhavgarh's compact valleys and grassy meadows make it one of the best places in India to see a tiger, and your naturalist shares the stories of the dynasties that have ruled these hills. Leopards, sloth bears and dhole are seen here too.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  4. Day 4: Bandhavgarh National Park

    A second full day in the forest. In the Tala zone, the track climbs beneath the sandstone cliffs of the ancient fort, past rock-cut caves to Shesh Shaiya, a reclining stone Vishnu watched over by langurs. In the evening, dinner is served by lantern light beneath the mahua trees.

    Overnight: Mahua Kothi (Taj Safaris), Bandhavgarh

  5. Day 5: Bandhavgarh to Kanha National Park

    After an optional dawn safari and breakfast, drive south for five to six hours to Kanha. Banjaar Tola stands on the banks of the Banjar River, its tented suites looking across the water to the park. Arrive in time for sundowners on your deck.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  6. Day 6: Kanha National Park

    Kanha's core is divided into four zones, Kisli, Kanha, Mukki and Sarhi, and your safaris move between them to reveal the park's full range. Morning mist lifts off the meadows to reveal herds of hard-ground barasingha, a deer Kanha saved from the brink of extinction, while gaur graze at the forest edge.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  7. Day 7: Kanha National Park

    Another day of morning and afternoon safaris, perhaps ending at Bamni Dadar, a high plateau with sweeping views across the forest at sunset. For dedicated wildlife lovers, a full-day permit allows uninterrupted time in the park from dawn to dusk, subject to availability.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  8. Day 8: Kanha National Park

    A final morning safari in Kanha. In the afternoon, visit a neighbouring Baiga or Gond village with a local guide, or learn the intricate style of Gond painting from a village artist. Your last evening in Central India is celebrated with a private dinner on the riverbank.

    Overnight: Banjaar Tola (Taj Safaris), Kanha

  9. Day 9: Kanha to New Delhi

    After breakfast, your chauffeur drives you to Jabalpur, about four to five hours away, for your flight to Delhi. Tonight you stay at Roseate House in Aerocity, a few minutes from the airport, ready for tomorrow's flight east.

    Overnight: Roseate House, New Delhi

  10. Day 10: New Delhi to Kaziranga National Park

    A morning flight carries you to Guwahati, gateway to India's North-East. Your chauffeur then drives you east along the Brahmaputra valley, past tea gardens and paddy fields, to Kaziranga. Diphlu River Lodge, with just twelve cottages raised on stilts above the river and paddy fields, is a world away from the city. Relax on your balcony as the light fades over the grassland.

    Overnight: Diphlu River Lodge, Kaziranga

  11. Day 11: Kaziranga National Park

    Set out at dawn into the Central Range, where the mist rises off tall elephant grass and shallow lakes known as beels. Greater one-horned rhinos graze in the open, often within sight of the jeep, alongside wild water buffalo, eastern swamp deer and herds of Asian elephants. In the afternoon, explore the Western Range at Bagori, with some of the park's highest densities of rhino and buffalo.

    Overnight: Diphlu River Lodge, Kaziranga

  12. Day 12: Kaziranga National Park

    Spend the morning in the Eastern Range, where wetlands draw pelicans, storks and fish eagles, and Kaziranga's tigers, among the densest populations in the world, may cross the track. In the afternoon, choose between a boat trip on the Brahmaputra in search of river dolphins or a walk through a local tea estate.

    Overnight: Diphlu River Lodge, Kaziranga

  13. Day 13: Kaziranga to New Delhi

    After a leisurely breakfast, drive back to Guwahati for your flight to Delhi. Your final evening is at The Oberoi New Delhi.

    Overnight: The Oberoi New Delhi

  14. Day 14: Departure

    Your chauffeur drives you to Indira Gandhi International Airport for your onward journey.

A note from our planners

The Madhya Pradesh reserves rest on Wednesday afternoons and close for Holi and Diwali, and Kaziranga is open to visitors from November to April. Your itinerary is arranged around these dates, with an in-lodge experience in place of any affected safari.

National Parks & Wildlife Reserves

  • Bandhavgarh National Park: Rugged hills, sal forest and grassy valleys beneath an ancient fort linked in legend to the Ramayana, with one of the highest tiger densities in India.
  • Kanha National Park: Towering sal forest and sweeping meadows in the Maikal hills, celebrated for saving the hard-ground barasingha and for excellent sightings of tigers, dhole and gaur.
  • Kaziranga National Park: A UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1985, Kaziranga's floodplain grasslands and wetlands on the south bank of the Brahmaputra hold the world's largest population of the greater one-horned rhinoceros. It was declared a tiger reserve in 2007 and has one of the highest tiger densities on earth.

Tiger & One-Horned Rhino Safari FAQs

Can I see both tigers and rhinos on one trip?

Yes. Bandhavgarh and Kanha are two of India's finest tiger reserves, and Kaziranga holds the world's largest population of the greater one-horned rhinoceros, which is seen on almost every safari there. Tigers are wild, so a sighting can never be guaranteed, but ten nights across the three parks give you excellent chances.

When is the best time for a tiger and rhino safari in India?

November to April, when both regions are open. Kaziranga welcomes visitors from November to April, while the Central Indian reserves are open from October to June. February to April is often the most rewarding, as the grass in Kaziranga is burnt back and animals in both regions gather near water.

How do I get from Central India to Kaziranga?

After Kanha, you fly from Jabalpur to Delhi, spend a night near the airport, and take a morning flight to Guwahati. From there, it is a scenic drive of four and a half to five hours to Kaziranga along the Brahmaputra valley.

Is this tiger and rhino safari suitable for families?

Yes. Jeep safaris suit most ages, and all three lodges welcome families with thoughtful activities for younger travellers. Our safari specialists can shorten the drives with an extra night or adjust the pace to suit your group.

How do I book a private tiger and rhino safari?

Speak with our safari specialists, who will tailor this itinerary around your dates, interests and pace. Every journey is private, bespoke and curated for you, and we recommend booking six to nine months ahead, as Diphlu River Lodge has only twelve cottages.
