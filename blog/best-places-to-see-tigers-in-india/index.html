Best Places to See Tigers in India

The best places to see tigers in India are Bandhavgarh, Kanha, Ranthambore, Pench, Tadoba, Corbett and Panna, each with its own landscape, character and rhythm. India shelters around 70 per cent of the world's wild tigers across 58 tiger reserves, so the choice is generous. The right park depends on what you wish to see alongside the tiger: ancient ruins, open meadows, Himalayan foothills or deep sal forest.

Bandhavgarh National Park, Madhya Pradesh

Bandhavgarh is celebrated for one of the highest tiger densities in the country, which makes it a natural first choice for guests who place sightings above all else. A 10th-century hilltop fort rises above the forest, and the landscape of rocky ridges, grassy clearings and bamboo is wonderfully photogenic. Allow at least four nights.

Kanha National Park, Madhya Pradesh

Kanha's sal forests and sweeping meadows are said to have inspired Kipling's The Jungle Book. Beyond tigers, it is a sanctuary for the rare barasingha, or swamp deer, as well as gaur, sloth bear and a rich variety of birds. It is among the most beautiful and best-managed reserves in Asia, and an elegant partner to Bandhavgarh.

Ranthambore National Park, Rajasthan

Nowhere else do tigers appear against such theatre: crumbling fortress walls, lakeside pavilions and banyan-draped ruins. Ranthambore is easily combined with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur, which makes it ideal for a first journey to India. Its tigers are famously confident, and the reserve has a long record of successful breeding.

Pench National Park, Madhya Pradesh and Maharashtra

The landscape that inspired Mowgli's jungle is a tapestry of teak woodland, rocky outcrops and the Pench River. Leopard, dhole and sambar are regularly seen, and the reserve is quieter than its neighbours, which suits travellers who value space and stillness.

Tadoba Andhari Tiger Reserve, Maharashtra

Tadoba is a favourite of experienced safari-goers, particularly from March to May, when its lakes draw tigers into the open. Its mix of bamboo forest, rocky hills and water offers dependable action, and it pairs beautifully with Pench.

Jim Corbett National Park, Uttarakhand

India's oldest national park, set in the foothills of the Himalaya, was the birthplace of Project Tiger in 1973. The river valleys here are also home to elephants and some of the country's finest birdlife. Corbett is a graceful choice for those who like their safari with mountain views.

Panna National Park, Madhya Pradesh

On the banks of the Ken River, Panna combines gorges, waterfalls and open forest with a growing tiger population. It is a rewarding extension to a central India route, especially for guests with an interest in photography.

How Many Parks Should You Visit?

We suggest two or three parks over ten to fourteen days, with four nights in each. A curated pairing such as Bandhavgarh and Kanha, or Pench, Kanha and Bandhavgarh, combines complementary landscapes and greatly improves the odds of a memorable sighting. Best Places to See Tigers in India FAQs

Which is the best national park to see tigers in India?

Bandhavgarh is renowned for its tiger density, while Ranthambore and Kanha are equally admired. The best choice depends on the landscape and style of journey you prefer.

Can I see tigers in the wild every time?

Sightings cannot be guaranteed, but spending four nights or more in each park and visiting two or three reserves gives you excellent chances.

Which park is best for a first visit?

Ranthambore is ideal, as it combines easily with Delhi, Agra and Jaipur and offers remarkable scenery.

Are these parks suitable for children?

Yes. All of them offer open-top vehicle safaris, and our specialists tailor the pace of each day for younger guests.

Can you build a private itinerary for me?

Absolutely. Every journey we design is private, bespoke and curated around your dates, interests and preferred lodges.
